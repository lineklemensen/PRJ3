# Conclusion

Throughout the development of the project, we have learned a great deal about many things. Firstly, the development of a product viable for use in real industries, and the precision that requires. Despite the goal being just a proof of concept, getting many different components, both soft- and hardware, developed by different people, to integrate seamlessly together is a big challenge, especially when we are still in the beginning stages of our software development careers, and working with Scrum, which we hadn't tried before. 

The importance of good communication, and putting in the time to make detailed diagrams during the architecture phase and sticking to said diagrams and our timeline became very apparent, especially towards the end of the project, when time became tight. One person forgetting or neglecting to do an assigned task, often has a lot of impact on other parts of the project, leading to issues piling up. Working with Scrum does combat this issue, however due to inexperience with the scrum workflow, and absense of the Scrum master, it was not properly taken advantage of. 

Another reoccuring issue was hardware. Our original H-Bridge breaking down and our two motors of the same model behaving in different ways, threw a bit of a wrench into our plans. However, since our financial budget has been close to non-exisistent, and having to use components supplied by the university, these issues were not directly our fault, and have given us valuable experience, when it comes to problem solving in stressful situations, concerning things we were not familiar with, even though it did hurt the quality of our final product.

Our final product, a proof of concept for a self driving delivery robot, reflects this. While the key functionalities are present, a lot of the minor requirements, such as things concerning accessability and quality of life improvements haven't been implemented. However, the functionality of such a system, that could be implemented in a hospital, has been proved to be possible. Even through our issues with communication and hardware, the system is still able to take a pair of coordinates, and convert that into a viable path and then traverse said path. Systems reminiscent of what we were aiming for, have already been tested across various hospitals across the country, further proving that a system like ours, with further development, could have real world applications that could easen the workload of an overloaded part of the health sector, helping improve workflows and ultimately help save lives.
